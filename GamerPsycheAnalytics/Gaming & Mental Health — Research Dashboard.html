Research Study · N = 13,377 Online Gamers · 11 Games

Does Gaming Affect Your
Mental Health & Wellbeing?

We surveyed over 13,000 online gamers about their gaming habits, personality, and life circumstances — then measured three things: how anxious they feel day-to-day, how comfortable they are in social situations, and how satisfied they are with their lives overall.

Using mixed effects statistical models (think of them as very thorough regression analyses that also account for which game people play), we asked: what factors actually predict better or worse mental health in gamers? The answers are sometimes surprising.

Rank is protective — skilled gamers are less socially anxious
🏆
Higher rank (−0.68 pts)
More skilled, higher-ranked players show significantly less social phobia. Gaming mastery may build social confidence, or socially confident people may invest more in competitive play.
🎓
Education (−2.19 pts)
The largest single effect in the social phobia model. Higher education strongly predicts less social phobia — a difference of over 2 points per education level.
⏱️
More hours (+0.07 pts/hr)
Playing more hours per week predicts slightly higher social phobia. Over many hours, this accumulates — someone playing 20+ hours may score noticeably higher.
📺
Streaming (+0.06 pts)
More streaming activity predicts higher social phobia — a counterintuitive finding given that streaming is inherently public.
➖
Narcissism — no effect here
Unlike anxiety, narcissism had no significant link to social phobia — suggesting the two mental health constructs have different personality drivers.

So what does this all actually mean?
Here's what the data consistently tells us across all models. These findings held up across 13,000+ participants and multiple statistical approaches.
Important caveat These are correlational findings — they show relationships, not causes. We can't say gaming causes anxiety, or that quitting gaming would improve wellbeing. The models explain only a small portion of variance in mental health (~2–3%), meaning many factors beyond what we measured also matter.
What consistently predicts WORSE outcomes
⏱️
More gaming hours
Linked to more anxiety, more social phobia, and lower life satisfaction — across every model we ran. The effect per hour is small, but consistent.
🛋️
Gaming to relax / escape
Players whose primary motivation is relaxation or escape show worse anxiety and lower life satisfaction. The motive matters, not just the behaviour.
📺
More streaming
Streaming gaming content is linked to higher anxiety and social phobia — possibly reflecting performance pressure or parasocial stress.
🎭
Higher narcissism
A personality trait linked to more anxiety — though not to social phobia or life satisfaction.
What consistently predicts BETTER outcomes
🎓
Higher education
The single most consistent protective factor across all three outcomes. Linked to less anxiety, much less social phobia, and greater life satisfaction.
💼
Employment
Being in work is the strongest predictor of life satisfaction in this dataset, and also linked to slightly lower anxiety.
🏆
Higher competitive rank
More skilled gamers show significantly less social phobia. Mastery and competence in gaming may translate into greater social confidence.
🎂
Older age
Older gamers report less anxiety and less social phobia — even after accounting for gaming behaviour and personality. Age brings resilience.
Summary
The big picture: real-world factors matter more than gaming factors
The most powerful predictors in this study — education, employment, and age — are not gaming variables at all. Gaming habits (hours, streaming) do predict outcomes, but with smaller effects. Which game you play is almost completely irrelevant (<0.3% of variance explained by game title).
